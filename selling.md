# Selling Your Home

## Sell with a plan that fits your home, your market, and what comes next.

Selling a home is not just about putting it online and waiting for offers.

The decisions you make before the home hits the market matter most. Pricing, prep, timing, presentation, offer terms, possession dates. They all need to work together toward the same goal.

The right strategy protects you. It shouldn't create extra work, extra cost, or extra stress.

## Start with why you're selling

Every sale starts from a different place.

Maybe you're moving up to a bigger home. Maybe you're relocating, downsizing, handling an estate, or selling a place that doesn't fit your family anymore.

The reason matters. It shapes how we plan the sale.

If you've already bought your next home, certainty and a reliable possession date may matter more to you. If you've got more flexibility, you can wait for better conditions. If you're selling and buying at the same time, we need to look at both moves together.

Before we talk paint colours, photos, or a listing price, I want to know what this sale needs to do for you.

## Understand the home before you plan the strategy

Buyers don't judge every home the same way.

A renovated family home on a quiet street draws different attention than an older place with redevelopment potential. A townhouse, an acreage, and a home near new construction each raise their own questions with buyers.

Your strategy should reflect:

- the condition and layout of the home
- the lot, location, and streets around it
- who's likely to buy this home
- what else is competing for that buyer
- recent sales and current market conditions
- the features that actually add value
- anything buyers might question or dig into
- your timeline and how much flexibility you have

The goal isn't to squeeze your home into some generic marketing formula. It's to understand what buyers are actually comparing it to, then position it right.

## Not every improvement is worth doing

Getting a home ready for sale can get expensive fast.

Some work helps. It makes the home easier to understand, more comfortable to walk through, more appealing to the right buyer. Other work costs more than it ever brings back.

Renovating isn't always the answer.

Sometimes a deep clean, some decluttering, a few small repairs, and better lighting is all you need. Other times, specific fixes help keep buyers from getting stuck on something small.

I'll help you figure out what's worth doing and what's simply going to cost you time and money without helping the sale.

Your home should look cared for. It should also look honest.

## Price shapes how buyers see your home

Price does more than decide where your home shows up in a search.

It influences who notices it, how buyers compare it to others, how quickly they act, and how confident they feel making an offer.

Price too high, and a good home can sit while buyers move on to something that looks like better value. Price too low without a real strategy behind it, and you might get plenty of attention but not the terms you wanted.

The right price depends on your home, the competition, how buyers are behaving right now, and how much certainty you need.

There's no single formula that works for every home.

My job is to show you the evidence, walk you through the trade-offs, and help you choose a pricing position you feel good defending.

## Presentation should make your home easy to understand

Good marketing helps buyers see your home clearly.

That can mean professional photos, floor plans, video, solid descriptions, the right online exposure, and showings that let people actually understand how the home works.

But presentation isn't about dressing things up or hiding the flaws.

Buyers respond better when the best features are front and centre and the home matches what they see when they walk through.

The goal is simple: get the right people looking, answer the questions they're going to ask anyway, and give serious buyers enough confidence to take the next step.

## Showing activity needs context

A packed open house doesn't automatically mean your home is priced right. A quiet week doesn't automatically mean the strategy failed.

Feedback, showing patterns, what else is on the market, how motivated buyers are, and the quality of the interest all need to be looked at together.

I'll help you understand what the activity is actually telling us.

Sometimes the right move is to stay patient. Sometimes the price, the presentation, the access, or the timing needs to change. That decision should come from evidence, not from reacting to every showing or comment.

## An offer is more than a price

The highest number on the page isn't always the strongest offer.

Conditions, deposit, financing, inspection rights, what's included, possession date, adjustment date, and the buyer's ability to complete the purchase all affect how strong the offer really is.

When offers come in, I'll help you compare:

- the price and the deposit
- how many conditions there are, and how serious they are
- the dates the buyer is proposing
- the strength and certainty of the buyer's financing
- the risk of delays or renegotiation
- how the terms line up with your next move
- what the offer is actually worth once you weigh it all

Negotiation isn't just about pushing for a higher number. It's about improving the result while protecting the chance the deal actually closes.

## Your next move can change the selling plan

If your sale is tied to another purchase, the two shouldn't be handled separately.

The best offer might come down to when you need the money, how much time you need between homes, whether temporary housing makes sense, and what your lender needs from you.

Possession dates can matter just as much as price.

A good selling strategy leaves your family room to make the next decision. It shouldn't force you into a home, a timeline, or a level of risk that doesn't sit right with you.

## Straight talk when the market shifts

Real estate rarely moves in a straight line.

New listings hit the market. Competing homes drop their price. Buyer confidence shifts. Interest rates, financing, and the time of year all move activity around.

You deserve to know when the plan is still working and when it needs a second look.

I'll tell you what's changed, what hasn't, and what your real options are.

Sometimes that means holding steady. Sometimes it means adjusting the plan. In some situations, it means deciding this isn't the right time to sell.

The advice stays useful even when the answer isn't the easy one.

## Advice that leaves room to wait

Selling can get emotional, especially when the outcome may affect what your family does next.

My job is to keep you clear-headed about the decisions in front of you.

I won't tell you every showing looks promising. I won't tell you to take every offer. I won't push work on you just because it's common practice, and I won't pressure you to sell if the plan no longer fits your family's goals.

You should understand what's happening, why I'm recommending it, and what might come next.

A good sale isn't just one that closes.

It's one you can look back on and feel good about every decision you made along the way.

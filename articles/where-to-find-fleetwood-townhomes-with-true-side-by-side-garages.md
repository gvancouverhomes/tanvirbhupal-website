Search for a townhouse in Fleetwood, and you'll run into this fast: "double garage" doesn't always mean what you think. A tandem garage parks one car behind the other. That's technically two spots, but it's not what most families actually need.

I've walked enough buyers through this exact problem to know the fix isn't luck. It's strategy. If you want a true side-by-side garage, you have to shift how you search. Layout and function matter more than shiny finishes.

## The Reality of Side-by-Side Garages in Fleetwood

Garage configuration varies quite a bit from one Fleetwood townhouse project to another. Tandem layouts are common, but newer doesn't automatically mean tandem, and older doesn't automatically mean side-by-side. If this feature matters to you, I would not use the age of the complex as a shortcut. Check the actual floor plan.

In my experience, established complexes from the 1990s and early 2000s can still be a useful place to look because some were built with wider units and more generous site layouts. I've also seen worthwhile options in larger established complexes around 168 Street and 84 Avenue and in older parts of Fleetwood near Fleetwood Park. That's a starting point for the search, not a guarantee. Garage layouts can differ even among homes that look similar from the street.

That distinction matters more than people expect. No shuffling cars around before work. More usable room for bikes, tools and gear. Nobody gets boxed in during the morning rush.

If you're coming from an apartment and trying to work out how much those practical differences actually matter, my guide to [moving from a Burnaby condo to a Fleetwood townhouse]({{ '/articles/moving-from-a-burnaby-condo-to-a-fleetwood-townhouse-space-vs-commute/' | relative_url }}) looks at the space-versus-commute trade-off more broadly.

## Understanding the Trade-offs

Going after a side-by-side garage means accepting a few possible trade-offs. Here's what I would keep in mind.

### Age of the Complex

If your search leads you toward an established complex, look beyond the garage itself. Roofing, exterior components, mechanical systems and other common property may be further along in their life cycle than they would be in a newer development.

That does not make an older townhouse a bad choice. It just makes the condition of the strata more important. My comparison of [older Fleetwood townhouses vs. new builds]({{ '/articles/older-fleetwood-townhouses-vs-new-builds-which-offers-better-value/' | relative_url }}) goes deeper into that decision.

### Modern Finishes

Older townhomes may not have the open-concept kitchens or smart-home features you'd find in new construction. But they can make up for that with bigger square footage, more generous room sizes, better storage or a layout that simply works better.

### Location Flexibility

True side-by-side garage layouts are not available in every Fleetwood complex. Your ideal school catchment or walking-distance pocket may not have the configuration you want, so you may need to compromise on location to get the garage.

That is why I would decide early whether side-by-side parking is a preference or a genuine requirement. It saves a lot of wasted showings.

### Strata Governance

Older complexes can carry different fee structures. Higher fees aren't automatically a red flag. They can reflect the real cost of maintaining a mature property.

In B.C., strata corporations with five or more strata lots are required to obtain depreciation reports on a five-year cycle. The Province explains that these reports project maintenance, repair and replacement costs for common property and assets over a 30-year period and include information about funding through the contingency reserve fund. You can read the current [B.C. depreciation report requirements](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/operating-a-strata/repairs-and-maintenance/depreciation-reports/depreciation-report-requirements){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" }.

So don't judge a townhouse by the monthly fee alone. Read the depreciation report, review the strata minutes and look at the contingency reserve fund in context. My guide to [Fleetwood townhouse strata fees]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-normal-strata-fee-for-a-fleetwood-townhouse/' | relative_url }}) explains how I would work through those documents.

Parking due diligence should go beyond your own garage too. If you regularly have family or friends over, take a close look at the visitor stalls, current rules and meeting minutes. I cover that separately in my guide to [visitor parking in Fleetwood strata complexes]({{ '/articles/the-visitor-parking-problem-in-fleetwood-strata-complexes-how-to-avoid-it/' | relative_url }}).

## Evaluating Your Long-Term Needs

Choosing between a tandem layout and a side-by-side unit really comes down to one thing: which daily frustration can you live with?

Ask yourself this. Five years from now, which would bother you more? An outdated kitchen you can renovate on your own timeline? Or moving two cars around every single morning just to leave your own driveway?

For a lot of families, the daily convenience of a side-by-side garage wins out, even if it means a longer search or setting aside a budget to update the kitchen down the road.

## Frequently Asked Questions

  <details>
    <summary>Is a "double garage" listing always a true side-by-side?</summary>
    <p>No. A listing may describe a two-car tandem garage as a double garage. Check the floor plan and listing photos rather than relying on the wording alone. For a true side-by-side layout, you want two vehicles able to park beside one another inside the garage.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>Do townhomes with side-by-side garages always have higher strata fees?</summary>
    <p>No. Garage configuration does not determine the strata fee. The more useful questions are what the strata is responsible for, what the operating budget covers, what the depreciation report identifies, how the contingency reserve fund is being funded, and whether significant work is approaching.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>Are there newer Fleetwood developments with side-by-side garages?</summary>
    <p>Yes. Newer does not automatically mean tandem. Garage configuration varies by development and sometimes by floor plan within the same community. If a side-by-side garage is non-negotiable, verify the specific unit rather than filtering only by the age of the project.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>How do I find out which Fleetwood complexes have side-by-side garages?</summary>
    <p>If you're working with a realtor, make the garage configuration part of the search criteria from the beginning. Then verify it against listing photos and floor plans before booking a showing. The words "double garage" alone are not enough.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>Is it worth compromising on location to get a better garage?</summary>
    <p>It depends on what matters most to your household. If you have two vehicles, need real storage and expect to use the garage every day, the layout can make a meaningful difference. But if you need to be close to a specific SkyTrain station, school or part of Fleetwood, you may decide that location matters more. I would make that trade-off consciously rather than discovering it after you move in.</p>
  </details>

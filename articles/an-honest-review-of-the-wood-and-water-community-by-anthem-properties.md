I've walked a lot of townhouse complexes in Fleetwood. Wood & Water is different, and buyers notice it fast.

[Wood & Water](https://anthemproperties.com/property/wood-water/){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" } is an Anthem Properties community at 15255 Sitka Drive in Fleetwood. Anthem describes the overall development as a mix of 325 duplexes, townhomes and apartments next to Sequoia Ridge Park, forested trails and Eaglequest Golf.

### Wood & Water at a glance

- **Developer:** Anthem Properties
- **Address:** 15255 Sitka Drive, Surrey
- **Community size:** 325 homes
- **Housing types:** Duplexes, townhomes and apartments
- **Area:** Fleetwood, Surrey
- **Nearby setting:** Sequoia Ridge Park, forested trails and Eaglequest Golf

Those are the development facts. What follows is my take on the things that are harder to understand from a project page or listing photos: how the community feels, how the townhouse layouts function, what the stairs are like in everyday life, and whether the parking setup works for the way you actually live.

Most people shopping for a townhouse here get stuck on square footage or finish quality. That's the wrong place to start. Wood & Water isn't like some of the tighter, higher-density development you see elsewhere around Fleetwood. It feels more open. Quieter. And that's exactly why you need to look past the listing and check if the layout actually fits how you live.

## The Community Feel: A Departure from Higher-Density Development

Fleetwood buyers worry about one thing more than almost anything else: feeling boxed in. In newer higher-density projects, streets and setbacks can be tighter and homes can sit closer together.

Wood & Water doesn't feel like that to me. Clients I've brought through often mention the same thing first: it's calmer here. Quieter. It still feels like a neighbourhood, not a stack of units.

That contrast is especially noticeable when you have been looking at development around Fleetwood's Fraser Highway corridor. The [Surrey Langley SkyTrain project](https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/stations/){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" } is adding 152 Street Station at Fraser Highway and 152 Street, along with Fleetwood Station at 160 Street and Fraser Highway. Keeping that changing corridor in context is useful when you're comparing how different Fleetwood townhouse communities feel today.

## Understanding the Layout: The Square Footage Reality

Here's where I tell every buyer to slow down. The number on the listing isn't the whole story.

### Garage and Entry Integration

A big chunk of the ground floor goes to the garage and entry. That's not wasted space if you want a real side-by-side garage. But it means your kitchen, living room, and dining area all sit on the second floor. Keep that in mind.

In my experience, true side-by-side garages are less common in Fleetwood townhouse complexes than tandem layouts. If parking configuration matters to you, my guide to [Fleetwood townhomes with true side-by-side garages]({{ '/articles/where-to-find-fleetwood-townhomes-with-true-side-by-side-garages/' | relative_url }}) explains what to look for before relying on the words "double garage" in a listing.

### The Staircase Factor

Because the main living space is upstairs, stairs become part of your daily life. Groceries, strollers, bad knees. If any of that applies to you, this matters more than the total square footage does.

Don't judge these homes by size. Judge them by how you'll actually use them. Want an open second-floor living space, a secure garage, and a quiet street? This layout works well. Need ground-level living? Look twice before you commit.

## Due Diligence for Prospective Buyers

A few things I tell every buyer to check before they sign anything:

- **Test the stairs.** Walk the route from the garage to the kitchen like you're carrying groceries or chasing a toddler. See how it feels.
- **Check the garage.** If you need room for two vehicles, storage or hobbies, make sure the specific garage layout really works for you.
- **Visit at night.** Go back in the evening when people are home. That's when you'll get a better read on parking, noise and how busy the complex actually feels.

And don't stop with what you can see during the showing. Review the strata documents too. The monthly number makes more sense once you understand what it pays for, how the complex is being maintained and what future work may be coming. My guide to [Fleetwood townhouse strata fees]({{ '/articles/what-is-a-normal-strata-fee-for-a-fleetwood-townhouse/' | relative_url }}) explains how I would put those numbers in context.

Visitor parking deserves the same attention. A few empty stalls during a weekday showing don't necessarily tell you what happens when residents are home. If guests matter to your household, read the rules, review the minutes and visit when the complex is busy. I go deeper into that in my guide to [visitor parking in Fleetwood strata complexes]({{ '/articles/the-visitor-parking-problem-in-fleetwood-strata-complexes-how-to-avoid-it/' | relative_url }}).

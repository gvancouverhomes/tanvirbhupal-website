[Holy Cross Regional High School](https://www.holycross.live/){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" } sits at **16193 88 Avenue**. [St. Matthew's Parish](https://saintmatthews.ca/){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" } is just down the road at **16079 88 Avenue**. Buy a home near either one, and you're in an older, family-focused stretch of Fleetwood, right along 88 Avenue between about 160 Street and 162 Street.

Holy Cross is an independent Catholic school, not a Surrey Schools catchment school. Admission depends on the school's own current policies and application process. St. Matthew's is a separate parish entity. The surrounding area may be described locally by these landmarks, but it is not an official City neighbourhood.

Most homes here are detached houses on regular suburban streets, loops, and cul-de-sacs. There's some townhome living too, like Ryan Villa near 88 Avenue. For families connected to Holy Cross or St. Matthew's, living nearby can make the daily routine convenient. But the location also comes with things worth checking carefully: school-related traffic, Sunday Mass activity, public-school catchments, 88 Avenue exposure, parking, and the future planning context around the specific property.

If you're still deciding how this pocket fits into Fleetwood as a whole, my guide to [whether Fleetwood is the right choice for your family]({{ '/articles/is-fleetwood-surrey-the-right-choice-for-your-family/' | relative_url }}) gives you the broader neighbourhood picture first.

## What the Streets Look Like Around 160 to 162 Street

Turn off 88 Avenue and you'll find an older Fleetwood layout. Bigger lots, mature trees, generous space between houses, and quiet side streets that end in loops or cul-de-sacs.

Most of the homes here were built years ago, during an earlier stretch of Fleetwood's growth. There's some multi-family housing too, like Ryan Villa near 160 Street, but much of the immediate area is still made up of established residential properties.

What I would not do is assume that today's housing pattern tells you exactly what the area will look like decades from now. Surrey is actively updating the [Fleetwood Plan](https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/fleetwood-land-use-plans/fleetwood-town-centre-plan){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" }, and the City says the updated plan will be considered in 2026. A land-use designation can indicate future development potential, but it does not mean an individual property will automatically redevelop.

If someone tells you a particular house is about to be rezoned for apartments, or that it can never be redeveloped, don't take their word for it. Check the applicable City land-use designation, zoning, property dimensions and any site-specific constraints. That's the only useful way to evaluate the exact property.

## School Catchments: What Buyers Get Wrong

Here's a mistake I see a lot: families think that buying a house across the street from Holy Cross gets their kids in. It doesn't work like a Surrey Schools catchment.

Holy Cross uses its own admission process. Its current [2026/2027 new-student application](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfH0TrlbT9AmCAiTpEuJwSt4TnW_34sI-0j2B3GFBIQMfMb-Q/viewform?usp=sharing){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" } specifically says that submitting an application does not determine acceptance. The application also asks families about religious affiliation and parish registration, and Catholic families are asked for consent to have family information shared with their pastor as part of the school's acceptance decision.

So living next door can be convenient, but proximity itself is not presented as an admission advantage.

Public schools are a different question. Catchments can change street by street in this part of Fleetwood, and the closest school is not necessarily the school assigned to a particular home. Before you buy, put the exact property address into the official [Surrey Schools School Locator](https://www.surreyschools.ca/demographicsandfacilitiesplanning/school-locator){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" } rather than guessing from the map.

If schools are a major part of your move, my guide to [comparing elementary schools in Fleetwood Surrey]({{ '/articles/how-to-compare-elementary-schools-in-fleetwood-surrey/' | relative_url }}) explains how I would approach the decision. For the public secondary side, my [Enver Creek Secondary vs Fleetwood Park Secondary catchment comparison]({{ '/articles/enver-creek-secondary-vs-fleetwood-park-secondary-catchment-differences/' | relative_url }}) also shows why you should never assign a school based only on the general neighbourhood.

When it comes to resale, I would separate convenience from market evidence. Being close to Holy Cross or St. Matthew's may appeal to some buyers, particularly families already connected to those institutions. That does not mean proximity automatically increases the property's value. The exact home, public-school assignment, street position, traffic exposure, condition, lot and broader market still matter.

## Traffic: Weekdays vs. Sundays

Most residential side streets here can feel very different from 88 Avenue itself, which is exactly why I would visit the property at the times your family would normally be coming and going.

Holy Cross publishes a campus traffic pattern for its school operations, so school arrival and dismissal are worth paying attention to if you're considering a home close to the entrances. Rather than assuming how much traffic reaches a particular side street, visit during the morning school period and again around dismissal. Watch the turning movements, where vehicles stop, and how easy it is to get in and out of the property.

Sunday is the other period worth checking. St. Matthew's currently lists several Sunday Masses, including services at **8:30 a.m., 10:30 a.m., 12:30 p.m., 5:00 p.m. and 7:30 p.m.** That means activity around the parish changes throughout the day rather than happening during one single window.

How much of that activity you actually notice depends on the exact property. A home directly on 88 Avenue or close to an entrance can have a different experience from one deeper inside an interior residential street. I would not assume either one is quiet or busy without seeing it yourself.

## Getting Around Without a Car

This isn't a walkable urban pocket. It's an established suburban neighbourhood. Transit runs through the broader 88 Avenue and 160 Street area, but for groceries, shopping, sports and many family errands, a car can still be the easier option depending on where the exact home sits.

Fleetwood Community Centre is also part of the broader family routine in this part of the neighbourhood. If recreation matters to your household, my guide to [what the Fleetwood Community Centre actually offers families]({{ '/articles/what-facilities-does-the-fleetwood-community-centre-actually-offer-families/' | relative_url }}) goes through the facilities and programs in more detail.

The bigger transportation change is happening south of this pocket along Fraser Highway. The [Surrey Langley SkyTrain project](https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/project-overview/){: target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" } is under construction, with **Fleetwood Station planned at 160 Street and Fraser Highway** as part of the eight-station Expo Line extension. The project's current anticipated in-service date is **late 2029**.

For homes around 160 to 162 Street and 88 Avenue, that creates potentially useful access to future rapid transit without the property necessarily sitting directly beside the station. But I would not use one blanket walking-time estimate for the whole pocket. The actual route depends on the exact address, pedestrian connections, construction conditions and where you enter the station.

It is also too early to say that every residential street here will remain insulated from future growth or station-related activity. That needs to be evaluated property by property against the Fleetwood Plan and the actual location.

## What Affects Resale Here

Where a house sits on the street grid matters, both for everyday living and for how future buyers may respond to it.

A home facing or backing directly onto 88 Avenue has a different location profile from a comparable home farther inside a residential loop or cul-de-sac. Traffic noise, headlights, driveway access and general road exposure may matter to buyers. I would treat those as property-specific marketability factors rather than claiming that every home on 88 Avenue sells for a fixed discount.

The same principle applies to being close to Holy Cross or St. Matthew's. For one buyer, walking to school or church could be a meaningful convenience. Another buyer may care much more about being deeper inside a quiet street. The feature has value when the right buyer values it, but that is different from saying the landmark itself creates a guaranteed price premium.

Some older Fleetwood homes also have existing secondary suites, or layouts that buyers may look at for suite potential. Before counting on rental income, verify what is actually authorized at the specific property, along with access, parking and the physical layout. A suite that works well on one lot can create a very different day-to-day setup on another.

These older homes can come with bigger yards and mature landscaping, which is part of the appeal. But budget for what comes with an older property too. Roofs, drainage, plumbing, electrical components, windows, heating systems and other major items need to be evaluated based on the actual home rather than its neighbourhood.

If you're moving to Fleetwood, don't count on having a new family doctor or nurse practitioner lined up right after you move in. If someone in your household needs a new primary care provider, the smart move is to register that person with the <a href="https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care/health-connect-registry" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Health Connect Registry</a>, then plan on leaning on community clinics, urgent care, pharmacies, or HealthLink BC services for a while.

Here's the good news. Fleetwood has pharmacies, dental offices, clinics, and other health-related businesses along its commercial corridors. Exact services, hours, appointment systems, and availability should always be checked before you go.

## Getting Attached to a Family Doctor in Surrey

Finding a family doctor or nurse practitioner in Fleetwood may take time. Provider availability changes, and the Health Connect Registry uses your location and current information to help find an appropriate match.

The way this works in B.C. is through the Health Connect Registry, run by HealthLink BC. If you don't have a family doctor or nurse practitioner, you can register with the registry. Your information is added to a community list that attachment coordination teams use to try to match you with an available provider.

A few things to know before you get started:

- **There's no set timeline.** HealthLink BC does not provide a firm estimate for how long matching will take. Provider availability, your community, and health-care needs can all affect timing.
- **You may be matched with a nurse practitioner.** Nurse practitioners can provide ongoing primary care, prescribe medications, order diagnostic tests, refer patients to specialists, and provide other care within their professional scope. You can read more about <a href="https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care/nurse-practitioners" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">nurse practitioners in B.C.</a>
- **Keep your file updated.** Keep your address, phone number, and relevant health information current so the attachment team can reach you and make an appropriate match.

## Same-Day Care, Walk-In Clinics, and UPCCs

For the smaller stuff, an ear infection, a sprained ankle, a rash, or a sudden fever, you'll want a plan before you just show up somewhere.

Community clinics may offer appointment-based, same-day, phone, video, or walk-in care for minor and non-urgent concerns, but the operating model varies from clinic to clinic. Hours and availability can change as well. Before travelling, check the <a href="https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HealthLink BC service directory</a>, the clinic's current booking system, or call reception directly.

Another option is an **Urgent and Primary Care Centre**, usually shortened to **UPCC**. A UPCC is a Fraser Health facility for non-life-threatening medical concerns that need attention within roughly 12 to 24 hours. It is not the same thing as a hospital emergency department, and it is not simply another name for a walk-in clinic.

Surrey UPCC options include:

- **Surrey-Whalley Urgent and Primary Care Centre:** Unit G2, 9639 137A Street. Urgent care is currently available seven days a week, including statutory holidays, from 9:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. Check the <a href="https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-at-Location/E/3/urgent-primary-care-centre---surrey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">current Surrey-Whalley UPCC information</a> before travelling.
- **Surrey-Newton Urgent and Primary Care Centre:** 6830 King George Boulevard. Urgent care is currently available Monday to Friday from 2:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m., and weekends and statutory holidays from 9:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. Check the <a href="https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-At-Location/0/1/urgent-and-primary-care-centre---surrey-newton" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">current Surrey-Newton UPCC information</a>.
- **Cloverdale Urgent and Primary Care Centre:** 5711 176A Street. Urgent care is currently available seven days a week, including statutory holidays, from 9:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. Check the <a href="https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-at-Location/5/8/Service---Cloverdale-Urgent-and-Primary-Care-Centre" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">current Cloverdale UPCC information</a>.
- **South Surrey Urgent and Primary Care Centre:** Unit 120, 1711 152 Street. Urgent care is currently available seven days a week, including statutory holidays, from 9:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. Check the <a href="https://www.fraserhealth.ca/Service-Directory/Service-at-Location/9/F/Service---South-Surrey-Urgent-and-Primary-Care-Centre" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">current South Surrey UPCC information</a>.

Which one makes the most sense depends on where you live in Fleetwood, your medical concern, and current access.

You'll generally need to register by phone or in person, and an urgent-care nurse will assess your concern and determine the appropriate next step. Registering doesn't guarantee an in-person appointment, and arriving at the centre in person doesn't mean you'll be treated faster. Fraser Health prioritizes people based on medical urgency.

## Where Fleetwood's Medical Services Are Located

Healthcare businesses and related services can be found along Fleetwood's major commercial corridors, including parts of Fraser Highway, 152 Street, and 160 Street.

Those corridors are useful places to check when looking for nearby pharmacies, clinics, dental offices, and other health-related services. Fleetwood Park Village is near Fraser Highway and 160 Street, but being close to a medical office or pharmacy does not mean a particular service will be available when you need it.

Clinics change their hours, providers, booking systems, and whether they can see unattached patients. Before you rely on any listing, check the <a href="https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">HealthLink BC service directory</a> or the provider directly to confirm what's actually available.

## How to Bridge the Gap While You Wait

While you're waiting for the Health Connect Registry to come through, there are several ways to keep your family covered.

**Keep your old doctor if you can.** If you're moving from somewhere like Burnaby, Coquitlam, or Vancouver, you may be able to stay with your current doctor for a while if the clinic agrees to continue seeing you and the travel is manageable. This isn't guaranteed, but it's worth asking before giving up an existing primary-care relationship.

**Ask your pharmacist.** B.C. pharmacists can assess and treat 21 minor ailments, prescribe contraception, and renew or adapt some prescriptions when appropriate. The Province explains the current services on its <a href="https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/health/accessing-health-care/pharmacy-services" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Pharmacy Services in B.C. page</a>.

**Call 8-1-1.** HealthLink BC gives you access to health navigation and registered nurses 24 hours a day. After assessing your concern, a nurse may refer you to a HealthLink BC physician if further medical assessment would help. Physician consultations are currently available by phone or video from 9:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m., seven days a week, when referred by a registered nurse. HealthLink BC explains the service on its <a href="https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/find-care/healthlink-bc-8-1-1-services/physician-services-healthlink-bc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">physician services page</a>.

**Keep your records handy.** Have digital or paper copies of your medical history, specialist letters, current prescriptions, and immunization records ready to go. It can make an unfamiliar clinic visit easier when the provider needs your background.

If you have a dog and enjoy walking outside, living near Tynehead Regional Park can be a great choice. The park has forest trails, fresh air, and lots of space to explore. Before you buy a home nearby, it helps to know what everyday life is really like.

## What Is It Like to Live Near Tynehead?

Tynehead feels more like a forest than a city park. It is a place many people visit several times a week. When I have been in the area, I often see people walking their dogs, runners on the trails, and families enjoying the park. It seems to be part of many local routines.

If daily walks matter to you, pay attention to which park entrance is most convenient from the home. Metro Vancouver identifies several access points, including the Serpentine Fields, Tynehead Hatchery, Serpentine Hills and Serpentine Hollow entrances. The 168 Street and 96 Avenue area provides access to the Serpentine Fields and Hatchery entrances. Homes farther south in Fleetwood are still close, but the park may be more practical as a short drive than a walk.

## The Benefits

### Lots of Different Walks

There are many trails to choose from. You can enjoy a short walk before work or spend much longer there on the weekend.

### Great for Active Dogs

Dogs that enjoy longer walks usually do well here. They get lots of new smells and places to explore.

## Things to Know Before You Buy

### The Trails Get Muddy

Tynehead is a natural park. After heavy rain, trails can become muddy and slippery, especially in the fall and winter. Waterproof boots and a towel in the car will probably become part of your routine.

Metro Vancouver advises visitors to use caution during rainy weather and stay on developed and signed trails. You can check the official <a href="https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/tynehead-regional-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Tynehead Regional Park information</a> before you go.

### Wildlife Is Part of the Area

Wildlife is part of the park. Metro Vancouver notes that bears, cougars and coyotes pass through Tynehead from time to time and advises visitors to keep children and pets close if wildlife is encountered.

For dog owners, that means staying aware of your surroundings and keeping your dog under control. Metro Vancouver's <a href="https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/Documents/tynehead-regional-park-brochure.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Tynehead Regional Park brochure</a> provides the park's current wildlife and dog guidance.

### Most of the Park Is On-Leash

Outside Tynehead's designated leash-optional area, dogs must be leashed. Metro Vancouver says the leash-optional area can be accessed from the Serpentine Fields and Hatchery entrances.

If a large off-leash space is important to you, visit the park before buying nearby. You can also [compare Tynehead with other green spaces and off-leash dog areas in Fleetwood]({{ '/articles/a-tour-of-green-spaces-and-off-leash-dog-parks-in-fleetwood/' | relative_url }}).

## Choosing the Right Area

There is no single best subdivision. What matters most is how convenient your home is to the park entrance you expect to use.

Homes around the 168 Street and 96 Avenue side of the park can put the Serpentine Fields and Hatchery entrances nearby. Homes farther south are still convenient, but the park may become a drive rather than something you walk to from home.

**Updated August 19, 2026:** Travel times are not guaranteed commute times. Traffic, incidents, weather, parking, construction, transfers, and transit service changes can materially change the trip. Before relying on any commute estimate, use [TransLink's current Trip Planner](https://www.translink.ca/trip-planner) and test the drive at the actual time you expect to travel.

If you're moving to Fleetwood and working in Downtown Vancouver, plan for a real commute. The exact time depends on where in Fleetwood you start, exactly where downtown you're headed, when you leave, how far you are from a bus stop, and how many transfers you need.

The important part isn't finding one perfect commute-time number. It's understanding which part of the trip creates the biggest trade-off for your routine.

If you're commuting five days a week, being close to the Fraser Highway transit corridor can matter a lot. If you're hybrid, driving or using a Park and Ride may give you more flexibility. Downtown parking costs, your work hours, daycare drop-offs or other family stops, whether you need the car during the day, and how much you mind transfers all factor in too.

That trade-off is also what lets you live deeper in Fleetwood's quieter residential streets.

## The Driving Reality

The usual drive from Fleetwood toward Downtown Vancouver heads north toward Highway 1, across the Port Mann Bridge and through Burnaby before entering Vancouver.

The time can change substantially depending on the day. Highway congestion, collisions, construction and the major interchanges through Coquitlam and Burnaby can all affect the trip.

There's another part buyers sometimes underestimate: getting from the house to the highway in the first place.

Depending on where you live, roads such as 152 Street, 160 Street, 96 Avenue and Fraser Highway can become part of your morning route before you ever reach Highway 1. School traffic, construction and the exact location of the property can make two Fleetwood homes with similar map distances feel very different during an actual weekday commute.

That's why I wouldn't judge a home by its estimated drive time on a quiet Saturday afternoon. Test the route when you would actually be going to work.

## The Transit Reality

As of August 2026, the Surrey Langley SkyTrain extension is still under construction, with a current anticipated in-service date of **late 2029**, according to the official [Surrey Langley SkyTrain project](https://www.surreylangleyskytrain.ca/).

Until the extension opens, many Fleetwood transit trips toward Downtown Vancouver still require a bus-to-SkyTrain connection.

The [503 Fraser Hwy Express](https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/503/direction/1/schedule) and [502 Fraser Hwy](https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/502/direction/1/schedule) both connect the Fraser Highway corridor with Surrey Central Station. From Surrey Central, the Expo Line runs west toward Downtown Vancouver.

TransLink's current station-to-station travel-time information puts **Surrey Central to Waterfront at approximately 39 minutes** once you're on the train.

The bus connection is therefore a major part of the decision.

Live a short walk from Fraser Highway, and that connection can be relatively straightforward. Live deep in a residential pocket, where you need a longer walk or another local bus before reaching the Fraser Highway corridor, and the overall trip becomes more complicated.

That's why commute planning in Fleetwood is really property-specific.

## The Hybrid Compromise: Park and Ride

Want the SkyTrain without depending on the bus for the first part of the trip?

A Park and Ride can be the middle ground.

TransLink's current [Park and Ride information](https://www.translink.ca/rider-guide/driving/park-and-ride) lists **Scott Road Station** as a dedicated Park and Ride location with **1,494 parking spaces**, a **$3 daily rate**, and a **$60 30-day rate**.

Scott Road Station is at Scott Road and King George Boulevard and connects directly with the Expo Line.

TransLink does **not** currently list Surrey Central or King George as TransLink Park and Ride facilities, so I wouldn't plan a daily commute around simply leaving your vehicle at either station and assuming comparable commuter parking will be available.

Scott Road gives you a more clearly defined option: drive part of the way, park, and take the Expo Line into Vancouver.

You still need to account for the Fleetwood-to-Scott-Road portion of the trip, parking availability and your final walk from the downtown station to work. But for some hybrid commuters, removing the local bus connection makes the trip feel more manageable.

## How Your Commute Dictates Your Fleetwood Location

Your work schedule should shape where you buy in Fleetwood.

Downtown five days a week?

Being closer to Fraser Highway and the transit corridor may matter more to you than having the quietest interior street.

Hybrid schedule, with only a couple of downtown trips each week?

You may be more willing to look deeper into established residential areas in exchange for a quieter street, more yard space or the type of home you actually want.

The Surrey Langley SkyTrain project adds another layer to that decision.

The Province currently identifies three future stations directly relevant to Fleetwood:

- **152 Street Station**, planned for the northwest corner of Fraser Highway and 152 Street
- **Fleetwood Station**, planned for the northeast corner of 160 Street and Fraser Highway
- **Bakerview-166 Street Station**, planned for the northwest corner of 166 Street and Fraser Highway, with an integrated transit exchange

You can see the official locations on the [Surrey Langley SkyTrain station page](https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/stations/).

Once the extension is operating, homes within practical walking or connecting distance of those stations will have a different transit relationship with the existing Expo Line than they do today.

That doesn't automatically make closer better.

Living close to a future station may improve transit convenience, but it can also mean being closer to Fraser Highway, construction activity, future redevelopment and a busier urban corridor.

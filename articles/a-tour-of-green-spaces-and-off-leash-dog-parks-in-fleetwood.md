Let me take you for a drive.

Every family I show around Fleetwood asks about the same two places first: Fleetwood Park and the Surrey Sport & Leisure Complex. That's natural. They're the big, visible landmarks, the ones that show up first in any search. And they're great, but they're not really where you'll spend your Tuesday evening. So let's drive past them for a minute and I'll show you where people actually live their lives.

## Fleetwood Park Is for the Big Stuff

Think baseball games, weekend tournaments, kids running around a real playground while you catch up with other parents on the sidelines. It's the place you load the family into the car for. Great space, well used, exactly what it's built for. The City of Surrey lists ball diamonds, sports fields, playgrounds, trails, tennis courts, gardens and a water playground among the amenities at <a href="https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/fleetwood-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fleetwood Park</a>.

But if you ask a longtime resident what park they use most, it's rarely this one. It's something two minutes from their front door. That's where pocket parks come in, and honestly, this is the part most buyers don't think to ask about.

These are the tiny green spaces tucked between houses, the ones that don't show up as highlights on any map. You'd never plan a trip to one. But if you live nearby, that little patch of grass becomes part of your daily rhythm without you even noticing. It's where you take the dog before work because it's faster than driving anywhere. It's where the kids burn off energy after dinner while you finally get five quiet minutes on a bench. It's where you run into the same neighbours enough times that you start knowing their names.

That's the stuff that makes a neighbourhood feel like home, and it happens in these small spaces, not just the big ones. ## Surrey Lake Park Has a Different Feel

Now, let's talk about Surrey Lake Park, at 7500 152 Street, south of Fraser Highway, because people usually have the wrong idea about it.

It's not hidden. It sits right on 152 Street, so you'll see it as you drive through. What sets it apart is the feel, not the location. It draws a calmer crowd, people going for a quiet walk rather than families racing to catch the start of a tournament. If Fleetwood Park is where you go for energy, Surrey Lake Park is where you go to slow down.

The City of Surrey describes <a href="https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/surrey-lake-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Surrey Lake Park</a> as a scenic natural area with a human-made lake and a 1.5-kilometre nature trail. Seasonal flooding can make trails inaccessible during the rainy season.

## If You've Got a Dog, There's More to Know

Many Fleetwood streets work well for regular dog walks, but that varies by the exact pocket, sidewalk connections and nearby roads. Some dogs also need more room to run. That's where Freedom Dog Park, at 15452 84 Avenue, comes in.

The City of Surrey identifies <a href="https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/freedom-dog-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Freedom Dog Park</a> as a designated off-leash park with more than 1.25 hectares of open space, trails and grassy areas. Most of the park is fenced, but the City notes that some areas are not. Walk the layout first if your dog needs a hard boundary.

Owners who use it aren't replacing their daily walk. They're adding to it, usually when their dog needs to really run.

For something longer and more scenic, a lot of Fleetwood residents head out to Tynehead Regional Park on weekends. It's north of Fleetwood rather than inside the neighbourhood itself, but close enough that it has become a regular option for many people living here.

It's a beautiful place for a longer walk with your dog, but I want to be clear about one thing: it is not an off-leash park across the board. Metro Vancouver identifies a designated leash-optional area at Tynehead Regional Park. Outside that area, dogs must be leashed. You can check Metro Vancouver's current <a href="https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/Documents/tynehead-regional-park-brochure.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Tynehead Regional Park brochure</a> before visiting.

## Don't Judge Green Space From the Map Alone

You'll pull up a satellite view and see green everywhere, and it looks fantastic. Then you drive over and find out some of that "park" is a drainage corridor or an environmental buffer with a narrow path and not much else. No playground, no open grass, just a strip of land doing its job quietly in the background.

It still counts as green space, technically, but it won't function like the neighbourhood green you were picturing.

So here's my advice, and I give this to every family I work with: if a park matters to your daily life, go walk it yourself at the time of day you'd use it. A quiet morning walk feels completely different from a busy Saturday afternoon. Fifteen minutes on foot will tell you more than a map search will.

The real question isn't how much green space is nearby. It's what your life would look like there. That answer is often found in the small, unremarkable spots, not only the ones with signs out front.

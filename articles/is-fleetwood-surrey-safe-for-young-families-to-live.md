Is Fleetwood Surrey a safe place to raise your kids? I get this question all the time from out-of-town buyers.

A lot of people start with a generic crime map or a headline about Surrey as a whole. I understand why, but I don't think that tells you enough about what it is actually like to live on a particular Fleetwood street.

I grew up in Fleetwood. I moved away as an adult and later chose to come back. Now I am raising my own family here. What matters to me when I look at a home for a family is much more specific: the street, the traffic, the walking routes, what is nearby, and what the current data actually shows.

## The Real Question Is Usually More Local Than "Is Fleetwood Safe?"

Fleetwood covers a large area, and one street can feel very different from another.

The Fraser Highway corridor is changing substantially. Construction is underway on the Surrey Langley SkyTrain extension, with future stations at 152 Street, Fleetwood at 160 Street, and Bakerview–166 Street. The project currently has an anticipated in-service date of late 2029.

The City of Surrey is also planning higher-density growth around these future stations. That means the parts of Fleetwood immediately around Fraser Highway will continue to become more urban over time.

That does not automatically make those areas unsafe, and I would not use proximity to SkyTrain as a shortcut for judging safety. It does mean I would evaluate a home near the corridor differently from a home several blocks into an established residential pocket.

For current crime information, I would also check the official [Surrey Police Service crime mapping and statistics](https://surreypolice.ca/crime-maps-stats) rather than relying on broad assumptions about Surrey. The crime map allows you to look at recent incidents geographically, which is much more useful when you are evaluating an actual property.

## For Young Families, Traffic Can Matter Just as Much as Crime

When I am looking at Fleetwood through the eyes of a parent, vehicle traffic is one of the first things I notice.

A home on an interior residential street, cul-de-sac or local loop can offer a very different day-to-day experience from a home directly on a major route.

152 Street and 84 Avenue are important north-south and east-west routes through the area. I personally would be much more cautious about a home where my kids would be stepping directly out toward heavy traffic than I would about a house a block or two inside the neighbourhood.

That does not mean every interior street is automatically quiet or every arterial-road property is unsuitable. You still need to visit the exact block.

I like to check:

- how fast vehicles actually move past the property
- whether the street is commonly used as a shortcut
- where children would walk to school or a park
- whether sidewalks are available
- the position of the driveway
- sightlines around parked vehicles
- nearby intersections
- how the street feels during the morning and afternoon rush

## Where Family Life Shows Up in Fleetwood

One of the reasons I like Fleetwood for families is that the community infrastructure is easy to see once you spend time here.

The [Fleetwood Community Centre](https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/recreation-facilities/fleetwood-community-centre) is at **15996 84 Avenue**, beside the Fleetwood branch of Surrey Libraries, Fleetwood Park and Francis Park. The City currently offers registered and drop-in programming there for different age groups.

[Francis Park](https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/francis-park), immediately south of the community centre, has open grass, a playground and a 300-metre walking loop.

[Fleetwood Park](https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/fleetwood-park), at **15802 80 Avenue**, has play areas, a spray park, gardens and nature trails.

And [Surrey Lake Park](https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/surrey-lake-park), at **7500 152 Street**, offers a 1.5-kilometre nature trail around the lake.

Those places do not prove that every Fleetwood property is a good fit for a family. They do give you useful anchors when you are looking at how a particular home connects to parks, recreation and everyday routines.

## How I Would Screen a Fleetwood Listing for Family Safety

If you are looking at listings online, I would not simply search for the word Fleetwood and assume every result offers the same environment.

Start with the exact address.

Open the map and look at whether the property sits on an arterial road, a collector road, an interior street or a cul-de-sac. Then look at the route your children would actually use to get to school, a park, a friend's house or the community centre.

If you are serious about the property, go there more than once.

Visit during a weekday rush hour.

Come back after school.

Walk the block instead of only driving it.

Stand in the driveway for five minutes and listen.

Look at the intersections your children would need to cross.

Then check recent incidents through the Surrey Police Service crime map.

Those observations will usually tell you much more than a generic neighbourhood label.

School location matters too. ## What About the SkyTrain Corridor?

This is probably the part of Fleetwood that will change the most.

The Surrey Langley SkyTrain extension will add 16 kilometres to the Expo Line between King George Station and Langley City Centre. The Fleetwood portion includes 152 Street Station, Fleetwood Station at 160 Street, and Bakerview–166 Street Station. Current project information says the anticipated in-service date is late 2029.

Construction is already visible along the corridor. The project's Fleetwood Flyer launching gantry is installing elevated guideway segments westward through Fleetwood toward 152 Street Station.

At the same time, Surrey's planning framework concentrates much of Fleetwood's future higher-density growth around the Fraser Highway station areas.

For a family buyer, I would treat that as a **location trade-off**, not a safety verdict.

Being close to rapid transit may become extremely convenient. Being directly beside a growing mixed-use station area may also mean more activity, traffic and development around you.

A quieter interior street may give you more separation from that activity, but you may have a longer walk to the train.

Neither is automatically better. It depends on what your family values.

## My Bottom Line

I would be comfortable raising a family in many parts of Fleetwood. I am doing exactly that.

But I would never tell a buyer that Fleetwood is simply "safe" and leave it there.

The better question is:

**Does this particular street, property and daily route work for your family?**

That is where I would focus.

Look at the road.

Walk the route.

Check the current police data.

Understand what is planned nearby.

And pay attention to how the location actually feels when you are standing there.

That is a much stronger way to make the decision than relying on Surrey's reputation, a neighbourhood label, or a single online crime map.

Grocery shopping in Fleetwood usually comes down to one thing: Fraser Highway. Between the SkyTrain construction and the regular traffic, that road can eat your whole afternoon. But not every grocery run in Fleetwood actually needs the highway. It depends on where you live and where you shop.

## Know Your Store, Know Your Route

Fleetwood has several full grocery options that shape most of the route choices in this article: Fresh St. Market, No Frills, Safeway, Save-On-Foods, and two Fruiticana locations. This isn't a complete list of every grocery business in the neighbourhood, but it covers the stores that most affect how you get around. Beyond those, there are smaller shopping options too, like Fleetwood Farm Market and a couple of Dollarama locations. They round out the picture, especially if you're trying to stay off Fraser Highway.

<a href="https://www.freshstmarket.com/contact/surrey" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fresh St. Market</a> sits at 15930 Fraser Highway, near 160 Street. The address faces the highway, but the access depends on where you're coming from. If you're south of the shopping centre, 158 Street may let you reach the shopping area without using Fraser Highway, depending on the current access pattern. Coming from other directions, you'll likely need to use or cross the highway, especially with construction shifting things around. Fruiticana Fleetwood, at 15933 Fraser Highway in this same shopping area, is another genuine grocery option here, especially for produce, spices, and international groceries.

Prakash's No Frills is at 15355 Fraser Highway, near 154 Street. It's also directly on the highway, but farther west than Fresh St. Market. For residents near 152 Street, 154 Street, or central Fleetwood, it can be a practical discount option without having to travel as far east toward 160 Street.

Safeway Fleetwood is at 8860 152 Street, near 88 Avenue. Save-On-Foods is close by, at 9014 152 Street, near 90 Avenue. Both sit off the highway, along the 152 Street corridor. Fleetwood Farm Market, which many longtime residents remember as Joe's Farm Market, is another useful stop along the 152 Street corridor, at 8932 152 Street. It's smaller than the main grocery stores, and better suited to fresh vegetables, fruit, and grocery top-ups than a full supermarket run.

There's also a second Fruiticana, Fruiticana Surrey Fleetwood, at 15230 84 Avenue, near 152 Street. For residents around 152 Street and 84 Avenue, or in southwestern Fleetwood generally, this is another option that may let you get your groceries without going anywhere near Fraser Highway. If you live in western or northwestern Fleetwood, that matters. You may be able to reach Safeway, Save-On-Foods, Fleetwood Farm Market, or the Fruiticana on 84 Avenue without touching Fraser Highway at all. Your trip may stay off the highway, though it can still involve busy roads like 88 Avenue.

Worth knowing too: there's a Dollarama right next to Fresh St. Market at 15950 Fraser Highway, and another at 9014 152 Street, in the same shopping area as Save-On-Foods. Neither one is a full grocery store, but they're handy for a quick top-up. Cleaning supplies, snacks, pantry basics, party items. A lot of Fleetwood families end up using one or the other as part of their regular routine.

## Route Strategy by Location

If you live near 152 Street and 84 Avenue, Safeway, Save-On-Foods, Fleetwood Farm Market, and the Fruiticana on 84 Avenue are all in the broader corridor. Depending on your exact starting point, you may be able to reach them without using Fraser Highway.

If you live between 152 Street and 156 Street, No Frills is also worth considering. Getting there will usually mean approaching or briefly using Fraser Highway, since the store sits right on it.

If you live near 160 Street, you're closest to Fresh St. Market, the Fruiticana beside it, and the Dollarama in that shopping area. Depending on your exact starting point and the current construction access pattern, you may be able to reduce how much time you spend on Fraser Highway. From other directions, some highway time is likely part of the trip. If you're considering this part of Fleetwood more broadly, my guide to [walking to shops and parks near Frost Road]({{ '/articles/can-you-really-walk-to-the-shops-and-parks-near-frost-road/' | relative_url }}) looks at the same area from a pedestrian perspective.

If you live farther east in Fleetwood, you have a choice to make. You can head west toward the 152 Street grocery cluster, or stay closer to home near the Fresh St. Market area. Neither one is automatically faster. It depends on traffic that day, and on which direction you're coming from.

Roads like 88 Avenue can help you cut down your time on Fraser Highway. But 88 Avenue is a major arterial itself, not a quiet backstreet. It carries a lot of traffic, especially during commuter hours. Using it can still mean congestion. It just moves the congestion to a different road. There's no guaranteed shortcut here. Just fewer or more direct ways to get stuck in traffic. If you're considering a home along that corridor, my guide to [living on or near 88 Avenue in Fleetwood]({{ '/articles/what-its-really-like-to-live-on-or-near-88-avenue-in-fleetwood/' | relative_url }}) goes deeper into the trade-offs.

## Watch the Clock

I've found the toughest window for driving in Fleetwood tends to run from about 3:00 PM to 6:00 PM. That's just my own experience, not a hard rule. Commuter traffic, school pickups, and construction crews often overlap during that stretch, and it can slow things down more than people expect.

The SkyTrain project is still active, and access points and lane conditions can shift. Before you head out, it's worth checking the current <a href="https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/category/traffic-advisories/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Surrey Langley SkyTrain traffic advisories</a>. What was true last month might not be true today.

## Why This Matters When You're Buying a Home

Most buyers look at how close a home is to the store. Few think about the route to get there. That's a mistake.

Two homes can sit the same distance from the grocery store and give you completely different experiences. One route might run through quiet, established streets. The other might dump you straight onto a construction zone. Same distance. Very different Tuesday.

What trips people up isn't the length of the drive. It's the unpredictability. A route that looks easy on a Saturday afternoon house tour can turn into a mess on a Wednesday at 4:30. If you've got kids, a job, and a schedule to keep, that unpredictability adds up fast.

That's also why I pay attention to the surrounding residential streets rather than judging a home by the neighbourhood name alone.

The City of Surrey does have official community-boundary information for Fleetwood. But City community boundaries, land-use plans, school catchments, MLS information, and everyday local identity serve different purposes, so they do not always line up neatly.

That is why buyers and sellers should look past the neighbourhood name in a listing and check the exact address.

The City describes [Fleetwood](https://www.surrey.ca/about-surrey/our-communities/fleetwood) as a community in northern Surrey, with Whalley to the west, Newton and Cloverdale to the south, and Guildford to the east and north. The Fleetwood planning area is described differently, extending from Green Timbers Urban Forest in the west toward the Agricultural Land Reserve in the east.

Those descriptions are useful for orientation, but neither should be used as a shortcut for deciding where one particular property belongs. Near Fleetwood’s outer edges, the correct answer depends on which boundary system you are checking.

## Why Fleetwood’s Borders Feel Unclear

Most buyers think a neighbourhood boundary should be one clean line. In practice, several different maps can apply to the same address.

The City has community boundaries.

Separate land-use plans guide future growth and development.

Surrey Schools draws catchment boundaries based on its own needs.

Real estate listings may also use informal neighbourhood wording to help buyers understand where a property is located.

Then there is the way residents actually experience the area. A family may shop, attend school, use parks, and spend most of its time in Fleetwood, even when a particular planning map places the property near another community.

None of these systems is automatically wrong. They are simply answering different questions.

## The Western Side Near Green Timbers

The City identifies Whalley as the community west of Fleetwood. Green Timbers Urban Forest is also an important landmark along this side of the area.

Properties near the western edge can feel connected to more than one part of Surrey because major roads, parks, schools, and shopping routes do not stop at a community boundary.

That does not mean the City’s boundary changes. It means daily life can feel less precise than the line shown on a map.

## The Southern Edge Toward Newton and Cloverdale

The City identifies Newton and Cloverdale as being south of Fleetwood.

This is where broad neighbourhood labels can become unreliable. A property may be close to Fleetwood Park, Fleetwood schools, or Fraser Highway businesses without necessarily falling inside every Fleetwood boundary used by the City, a planning document, or another organization.

Rather than assuming that a nearby landmark decides the neighbourhood, check the exact address against the map or system that matters to your decision.

## The Eastern and Northeastern Side

Guildford lies to the east and north of Fleetwood.

The northeast side also includes the [Anniedale-Tynehead planning area](https://www.surrey.ca/renovating-building-development/land-planning-development/land-use-planning/guildford-land-use-plans/anniedale-tynehead-ncp). The City handles the Anniedale-Tynehead Neighbourhood Concept Plan within its Guildford land-use planning materials.

The plan area is generally located east of 168 Street, with Highway 1 and 96 Avenue toward the north, the Agricultural Land Reserve toward the south, and Harvie Road toward the east.

Those general reference points do not prove that a particular property falls inside the plan. Buyers should confirm the exact parcel using the official plan map or the City’s COSMOS mapping system.

## Berkshire Park and the Northern Fleetwood Area

[Berkshire Park](https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/berkshire-park) is an official City park at 9283 154 Street, and the City identifies it as being in Fleetwood. Berkshire Park Elementary is also an official school in Fleetwood.

The park and school are useful local landmarks, but “Berkshire Park” is not a formally bounded neighbourhood on its own.

When someone refers to homes around Berkshire Park, they are usually describing proximity to the park or school, not identifying a separate official community. The exact address should still be checked before making conclusions about City boundaries, school catchments, zoning, or planning areas.

## Listing Descriptions Versus Official Classifications

A listing description may use a familiar neighbourhood name to help buyers understand the property’s general location.

That wording is not necessarily the same as an official City boundary, planning designation, school catchment, or formal real estate subarea field.

For example, an agent might describe a home as being near Fleetwood because it is close to Fleetwood Park, local schools, or Fraser Highway. That may be useful context, but it should not replace an address-level check.

The practical lesson is simple: treat neighbourhood wording as orientation, not proof.

## Why School Catchments Are a Separate Question

Surrey school catchments do not simply follow community or real estate boundaries.

A property near the edge of Fleetwood may be close to one school while being assigned to another. Catchments can also change as enrollment and school capacity change.

Do not rely on a listing description, the nearest school, or what happened with a neighbouring property several years ago.

Use the [Surrey Schools School Locator](https://www.surreyschools.ca/demographicsandfacilitiesplanning/school-locator) and enter the exact address.

## How the Fleetwood Plan and SkyTrain Fit In

The Fleetwood Plan is the City’s long-term land-use planning process for the area. The City says the plan will guide growth around Fleetwood’s three Transit-Oriented Areas and in the surrounding community.

Surrey has also designated Transit-Oriented Areas under provincial requirements around applicable SkyTrain stations. These areas and the Fleetwood Plan are connected parts of the broader planning picture, but they are not the same thing.

Being inside a planning boundary or Transit-Oriented Area does not guarantee that an individual property can be redeveloped.

Actual development potential depends on current zoning, the parcel’s size and shape, access, servicing, frontage, applicable City policies, and whether neighbouring properties need to be assembled.

For buyers and homeowners, the map is the beginning of the investigation, not the final answer.

## How to Check a Fleetwood Border Property

If you are considering a property near Fleetwood’s edges, check each question separately.

### Check the City Boundary

Use the City of Surrey’s community-boundary information or COSMOS to see how the address is mapped.

### Check the Applicable Land-Use Plan

A property may fall inside the Fleetwood Plan, the Anniedale-Tynehead plan, or another planning area. Confirm it using the official plan map rather than relying on a nearby road or landmark.

### Check the School Catchment

Enter the exact address into the Surrey Schools locator. Do not assume that being described as Fleetwood guarantees admission to Fleetwood Park Secondary or any particular elementary school.

### Check Zoning and Development Information

COSMOS is a useful starting point for reviewing mapped zoning, infrastructure, community boundaries, planning information, and certain rights-of-way.

It is not a complete legal investigation. Title charges, legal rights-of-way, current application status, and other parcel-specific matters may require confirmation through City records, land title records, a lawyer, a surveyor, or another appropriate professional.

### Visit the Street

Maps cannot tell you everything about daily life.

Drive the area during the morning and evening. Look at traffic flow, access to Fraser Highway, walking routes, nearby construction, transit connections, and how the street relates to the places your family will actually use.

A community name can help you get your bearings. But when it comes to boundaries, planning areas, schools, zoning, development applications, and property rights, the exact address is what settles the question.

If you're looking at a map of Surrey, 88 Avenue might look like just another residential road. It's not. This is one of Fleetwood's main east-west routes. Living near it gives you great access to everything. Living right on it means dealing with some real trade-offs every single day. Both matter when you're figuring out what a home is actually worth.

## The Frontline Reality: On the Avenue vs. Inside the Subdivision

There's a big difference between a home that fronts 88 Avenue and one sitting just a block or two behind it.

### Fronting 88 Avenue

Homes facing the avenue get hit with steady commuter traffic, especially during morning and afternoon rush hour. That comes with a few specific problems.

Noise. It's not just engine hum. You get buses, trucks, cars accelerating away from lights, and the sound of hard braking.

Headlights. At night, headlights from passing cars shine straight into front-facing rooms.

Daily life. Good windows and solid renovations can cut down the noise inside your house. But they won't change what it's like to open a window in summer, use your front yard, or back out of your driveway into moving traffic.

The City of Surrey classifies 88 Avenue as an arterial road in planning documents for properties along the corridor. That matters because arterial roads are designed to carry more traffic than the local residential streets around them.

### Tucked Inside the Neighbourhoods

Once you move onto the parallel streets, things quiet down fast. Streets like 87 Avenue, 86 Avenue, 88A Avenue, and 90 Avenue sit away from the main intersections, and homes there feel noticeably calmer. They're not exposed to the constant traffic stream the way avenue-front homes are.

But the street name alone doesn't tell the whole story. A home on 86 Avenue near 160 Street can still get turning traffic and through traffic. A cul-de-sac the same distance from 88th might feel completely different. Before you assume one block is enough of a buffer, go stand outside during afternoon rush hour and see for yourself. Farther north, around 92 Avenue, the traffic matters less to daily life, though it still depends on how close you are to the big crossings at 152, 160, 164, and 168 Streets.

The valuation rule is simple: a home fronting 88 Avenue can't be priced the same as an identical home sitting safely inside the neighbourhood. The same street-level thinking applies to other major routes, which is why my guide to [living on or near 156 Street]({{ '/articles/is-156-street-a-safe-quiet-road-for-your-kids-to-play-on/' | relative_url }}) focuses on the exact block rather than treating the whole road the same way.

## Pedestrian Practicality and the Catchment School Run

88 Avenue has sidewalks, transit stops, and traffic lights. But walking it isn't relaxing. Cars move fast between signals, and some crossings feel long and exposed, especially if you've got a stroller, young kids, or an older family member with you.

### The Catchment Locator Rule

School catchments around the 88 Avenue corridor change by address. It's not one clean line. Because of this, don't trust a listing that says a home is "near Coyote Creek" or in the "Walnut Road area." Type the exact address into the official <a href="https://www.surreyschools.ca/demographicsandfacilitiesplanning/school-locator" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Surrey Schools School Locator</a> and find out which elementary and secondary school it's actually assigned to.

### Looking at the School Routes

The hardest school run isn't always about which school your kids attend. It's about which side of 88 Avenue your house and your school fall on. A school might look close on a map, but crossing a major road with young kids changes your whole morning.

Frost Road Elementary and Maple Green Elementary are both near different parts of the 88 Avenue corridor. Frost Road sits near 164 Street beside Frost Road Park, while Maple Green is on the western side closer to 150 Street. Coyote Creek Elementary and Walnut Road Elementary also serve parts of Fleetwood, but whether any of these schools applies to a particular home depends on the exact address.

Before you assume a home is an easy walk to school, check the exact catchment with the address locator and walk the route yourself at drop-off time. Staying on the same side of the avenue as your school can make a real difference every morning.

## The Transit Advantage: Connecting to the Surrey Langley SkyTrain

Transit is one of the strongest reasons to buy near 88 Avenue.

Right now, the <a href="https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/320/direction/0/schedule" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">320 bus</a> runs through Fleetwood, including a stop at 88 Avenue and 156 Street, and connects Surrey Central Station with Langley Centre.

That's set to improve further. The <a href="https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/project-overview/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Surrey Langley SkyTrain project</a> currently has an anticipated in-service date of late 2029. That's the current project timeline, and it could still shift as the project moves forward.

88 Avenue connects directly to major north-south streets like 152 Street and 160 Street. The official <a href="https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/stations/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">station plan</a> confirms Fleetwood Station at 160 Street and Fraser Highway, along with an integrated transit exchange for north-south bus connections at Bakerview-166 Street Station. The final feeder bus routes, frequencies, and travel times from individual sections of 88 Avenue haven't been established yet.

You won't be able to walk to a SkyTrain platform from every block near 88th. Once the line opens, better connections into these stations are a reasonable advantage to expect given how integrated the avenue already is with the local road and bus network. But exactly how quick or direct that connection will feel from any given block isn't confirmed yet.

The SkyTrain line itself follows Fraser Highway, not 88 Avenue. ## How 88 Avenue Changes From West to East

88 Avenue doesn't feel the same all the way through. It changes as you move east.

### Near 152 Street

This is the western side of Fleetwood. It feels more urban and busier than the interior residential pockets farther east. It's a good fit if you want fast access to major routes and transit and don't mind trading some quiet for convenience.

### Between 156 Street and 160 Street

This is the core of Fleetwood. You get a mix of established homes, schools, parks, and amenities. It's busy on the avenue itself and noticeably quieter once you step behind it. For many buyers, this stretch gives you a practical balance between access and residential feel.

### East of 160 Street

The area becomes greener and more spread out as you move east. You start getting into older, established residential streets with mature trees and larger properties in some pockets. It's a good fit for buyers who want more of that established suburban feel, although daily errands can take a bit more planning depending on the exact address.

## Suburban Convenience Over Urban Walkability

If you're moving from somewhere like Vancouver or Burnaby, set your expectations right. 88 Avenue isn't a walkable main street lined with shops and cafés.

What it offers instead is convenience. You can get to major shopping areas quickly without winding through slow side streets. The commercial areas around the western side of Fleetwood, the shops near Fleetwood Village, and the Fresh St. Market near 160 Street and Fraser Highway are all generally a short drive or bus trip away, though the actual time depends on which section of 88 Avenue your home is near.

It's convenient. But you'll still be driving or taking transit for most errands. That's just how this corridor works. ## Pocket Park Escapes: Leaving the Arterial

The quiet spots around here aren't on 88 Avenue. They're tucked just behind it.

<a href="https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/frost-road-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Frost Road Park</a>, at 8607 164 Street, is one of the strongest park options near the eastern stretch of the avenue. It sits beside Frost Road Elementary and has a playground, a soccer field, open green space, and walking paths.

<a href="https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/bucci-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Bucci Park</a>, at 8594 166 Street, is a smaller neighbourhood park just south of 88 Avenue, with a playground, trails and natural areas.

<a href="https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/evergreen-tot-lot" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Evergreen Tot Lot</a>, at 15112 87B Avenue, sits close to 88 Avenue but feels completely removed from it once you're there. It's a good example of how fast things quiet down once you step off the main road.

<a href="https://www.surrey.ca/parks-recreation/parks/maple-green-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Maple Green Park</a>, at 8959 150 Street, sits just north of 88 Avenue and gives families on the west side another playground option along with courts, trails and open space.

A couple of other green spaces are worth knowing about, even if they're not directly connected to 88th. Freedom Dog Park is an official off-leash dog park in Fleetwood, but you'll need to walk or drive a short distance to reach it. Tynehead Regional Park, to the northeast, is a large regional park with dog areas, reached from 168 Street or 96 Avenue rather than straight off 88 Avenue.

## The Mature Canopy: Storms and Infrastructure

Some people assume older neighbourhoods near 88 Avenue lose power more often in winter storms than newer ones. There's no public data that backs that up.

Instead of worrying about the neighbourhood as a whole, look at the specific property. Check two things during your due diligence:

- **Electrical service:** Is it overhead wiring or underground?
- **Tree canopy:** Are there big, mature trees hanging over the roofline or the service line? Have the trees been trimmed or maintained?

Those big old trees are part of what makes older Fleetwood feel the way it does. People love them for the character and privacy they give. They just need a bit more upkeep than a brand-new lot with underground wiring and no mature trees yet.

## The Verdict: The Sweet Spot

The real advantage of living near 88 Avenue is how efficiently it connects you to everything else. It gets you quickly toward Highway 1, Tynehead, Fraser Highway, and Langley. One parent can commute toward Vancouver while the other heads south toward the future SkyTrain line, and the location can still work for both.

The sweet spot is buying close enough to 88 Avenue that you're a couple of minutes from the transit and connectivity, but far enough inside the subdivision that the traffic stays outside your living room.

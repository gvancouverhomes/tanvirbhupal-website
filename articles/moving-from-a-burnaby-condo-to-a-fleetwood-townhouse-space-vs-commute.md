I've sat across the table from a lot of Burnaby condo owners making this exact move. For buyers who currently live close to rapid transit, with a genuinely easy commute, the trade is simple to say and hard to live through: you give up some of that convenience for room to breathe.

Nobody wakes up one day and decides to leave Burnaby. It builds. The second bedroom turns into a nursery and an office at the same time. The dining table disappears under a laptop and cables. The stroller lives in front of the door because there's nowhere else to put it.

At some point, buyers notice they're organizing their whole life around a condo's limits. Moving to Fleetwood isn't just about what your budget buys per square foot. It's about separate bedrooms. A real workspace. Storage that isn't a bike locker three floors down. A yard the kids can actually play in. The longer commute only makes sense once daily sanity starts to matter more than living next to the SkyTrain.

## The Reality of the Commute

If you're used to walking out your door and straight onto a train platform, the Fleetwood commute will take some getting used to. This isn't a small inconvenience. It changes the shape of your whole day.

Until the [Surrey Langley SkyTrain extension](https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/project-overview/) opens, transit trips from Fleetwood toward Vancouver generally still require a bus connection to the existing Expo Line. The [503 Fraser Highway Express](https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/503/direction/1/schedule) is one of the main routes along Fraser Highway and serves King George Station and Surrey Central Station. How smooth the trip feels depends on where you live, how close you are to a useful stop, your departure time and how the connections line up.

If you want to look at the commute in more detail before deciding, my guide to [commuting from Fleetwood Surrey to Downtown Vancouver]({{ '/articles/commuting-from-fleetwood-surrey-to-downtown-vancouver-dated-car-and-transit-scenarios/' | relative_url }}) breaks down the car and transit trade-offs.

Buyers heading downtown five days a week tend to struggle more with this change. Many of them narrow their search toward the Fraser Highway corridor to protect what transit convenience they can. Hybrid workers, the ones only going into the office two or three days a week, are usually much more relaxed about a bus transfer or a short drive. They've made peace with the trade already.

## Choosing the Right Micro-Neighbourhood

Most Burnaby buyers start their Fleetwood search with a transit-first mindset. They gravitate toward the Fraser Highway corridor near the future Fleetwood Station at 160 Street and Fraser Highway, the area around Frost Road, or homes close to the future SkyTrain line. It feels familiar. It feels safe.

Then they start touring homes, and the decision turns emotional. They walk into a quieter complex near Fleetwood Park or nearby [Tynehead Regional Park](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/tynehead-regional-park). The floor plan is bigger. The trees are mature. Visitor parking may be easier. There's a real side-by-side garage instead of a squeeze. All of a sudden, a slightly longer walk to the bus stop feels like nothing.

What buyers want to avoid is ending up with the worst of both worlds, a townhouse that's still cramped, still stuck next to traffic noise, and still comes with a long commute. If you're leaving Burnaby, the new place needs to earn it. More space. More quiet. That's the deal.

## The Adjustment to Stairs and Parking

A townhouse isn't just a bigger condo. It works completely differently. Two things catch former condo owners off guard every time: stairs and tandem parking.

In a high-rise, everything happens on one floor. Groceries go from the parkade to the elevator to the kitchen, no stairs involved. In many Fleetwood townhouses, you'll walk in through the garage, carry groceries up one flight to the main living area, then climb another flight to reach the bedrooms and laundry. Some families like having that separation between floors. Others realize pretty fast that hauling a stroller, a laundry basket, and a toddler up two flights of stairs isn't the life they signed up for.

Tandem garages, where one car parks behind the other, cause a similar reaction. If you only have one car, a tandem garage can be a win. Park up front, use the back half for a home gym, workshop, or storage. But with two cars and overlapping morning schedules, you can end up shuffling vehicles just to get the blocked-in car out. That gets old fast, especially in complexes with limited or tightly controlled visitor parking.

If garage layout is high on your list, my guide to [Fleetwood townhomes with true side-by-side garages]({{ '/articles/where-to-find-fleetwood-townhomes-with-true-side-by-side-garages/' | relative_url }}) goes deeper into that trade-off.

There is also a strata-document side to parking that is easy to overlook. Parking stalls in BC strata properties can be designated in different ways, so before you buy, confirm exactly what parking comes with the unit and how it is allocated. The Province of BC has a useful overview of [strata parking stalls and storage lockers](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/housing-tenancy/strata-housing/renting-buying-selling/buying-and-selling-strata/parking-and-storage).

Before you buy, picture a normal Tuesday morning. Who leaves first. Where the garbage bins go. Where the hockey bag ends up. That's how you know if a layout actually works for your family.

## Trading Urban Amenities for Practical Lifestyle Space

Moving to Fleetwood can mean giving up some of the things condo living makes easy: a building gym, an elevator, common amenities, or having coffee and shops right outside your door.

A lot of the buyers I've worked with eventually realize they weren't using every building amenity enough for it to outweigh the space they were missing at home.

Instead of chasing the same urban walkability, the buyers who do well here look for space that actually gets used. A private, fenced backyard often becomes a priority. Letting the dog out without a leash. Hosting a barbecue without booking a strata common room. That's often a big part of why they left Burnaby in the first place.

Depending on the complex, you may not be walking to a café every morning. But nearby parks and trails give you a different kind of everyday convenience. The families who thrive in Fleetwood are the ones who make peace with that trade: less immediately on your doorstep, more room to actually live.

The age and design of the townhouse also matter. Some older complexes offer larger rooms, different garage layouts and more outdoor space, while newer projects may trade some of that space for newer finishes and a different maintenance profile. ## Frequently Asked Questions

  <details>
    <summary>Will the Surrey Langley SkyTrain extension completely fix the Fleetwood commute?</summary>
    <p>No single transit project will make every Fleetwood commute the same. The Surrey Langley SkyTrain extension is currently anticipated to enter service in late 2029 and will include 152 Street Station, Fleetwood Station and Bakerview–166 Street Station in and around Fleetwood. For people living near those stations, many trips should become simpler because they will be able to join the Expo Line without first taking a bus along Fraser Highway. Actual travel time will still depend on where you start, your station access and where you are going.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>Are side-by-side garages hard to find in Fleetwood townhomes?</summary>
    <p>They are available, but garage layout varies significantly by complex. In the Fleetwood listings I've reviewed, side-by-side garages show up more often in some older or lower-density complexes, while tandem layouts appear frequently in newer townhouse stock. If a side-by-side garage is non-negotiable, confirm the actual floor plan rather than relying only on a listing that says "double garage."</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>Do I need two cars to live in a Fleetwood townhouse?</summary>
    <p>Not necessarily. A one-car household can work in parts of Fleetwood, especially if the home is close to Fraser Highway transit and your regular errands fit the surrounding area. Other households find two vehicles much easier for work, school, recreation and errands. The answer depends more on the exact complex and your weekly routine than on Fleetwood as a whole.</p>
  </details>

  <details>
    <summary>Do all Fleetwood townhouses have private, fenced backyards?</summary>
    <p>No. Outdoor space varies significantly by development and even by unit. Some older complexes have usable fenced yards, while some newer or higher-density projects use smaller patios, balconies or rooftop spaces instead. If a real backyard matters for young children or pets, confirm the unit's outdoor space and strata rules before you buy.</p>
  </details>
